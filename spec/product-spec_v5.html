Document Purpose

This document is the product source of truth for ReferralAI: the domain model, the actors, the business rules and the behaviours every other document implements. It is written so that each rule can be read on its own — what the rule is, why it exists, how it fits with its neighbours, and what it looks like on a concrete example — and so that the companion documents (API, Event Model, DB, Architecture) can be checked against it line by line.

Key Constraints

These constraints are immutable. Every rule in the document is consistent with them, and a proposal that violates one is out of scope for V1.

No participant platform accessParticipants and invitees never log into the platform and there is no platform-hosted portal. All participant interaction happens through referral links, the in-app SDK widget and emails.
Selective, backend-owned enrollmentOnly people the client explicitly enrolls become participants. The platform never enrolls anyone on its own initiative and the browser cannot enroll anyone.
Enrollment-time variant resolutionThe variant (and therefore the reward) is resolved once, when a person is enrolled, and frozen. A participant who reads "earn €50" earns €50.
EU-first complianceGDPR, ePrivacy, consent management and EU data residency are product features, not afterthoughts.
API-firstThe dashboard consumes the same public API as clients. There is no private UI backend.
AI with human controlAI output is advisory by default; the two autonomous paths (bounded incentive tweaks, high-score fraud auto-block) are narrow, logged and reversible.
Fixed technical stackNine NestJS services, PostgreSQL per service, ClickHouse, Redis, SNS/SQS, Temporal, Ory, AWS eu-central-1.

Rule prefixes: CON concepts · DOM domains · ACT actors & permissions · SEG segmentation & eligibility · CMP campaigns & pulses · PRT participants & trust · RWD rewards · SDK tracking & SDK · ATT attribution · AI AI features · CPL compliance · INT integrations.

Vocabulary discipline. The document uses one word per concept and never switches between synonyms. In particular: the person who refers is always a Participant; the person who is referred is always an Invitee; an external fact entering the platform is a Tracked event; the subset of tracked events that describe the referral journey are Touches. The API path segment /v1/referrers is a wire name and is the only place the word "referrer" survives.

Phase tags. MVP ships at launch; Lot 1 and Lot 2 follow in 2027 H1; V2 is 2027+ and includes partner referral programs, which are entirely out of this document's scope.

1. Product Context

ReferralAI sells to B2B software companies that already have paying customers and want those customers to bring in the next ones. The product's job is narrow and measurable: turn an existing customer base into a tracked, fraud-resistant, revenue-attributed referral channel — without asking the client's customers to log into anything new.

2. Core Concepts & Glossary

The domain has three layers. Structure is what the client configures (Program, Campaign, Variant, Segment, Pulse, Playbook). People are the two external roles a person can hold (Participant, Invitee). Facts are what the platform records as it runs (Tracked events — of which Touches and Conversions are the two important classes — Referrals, Attribution results, Rewards, Payouts). Every term below is defined once; the rest of the document uses exactly these words.

Structure — What the Client Configures

Tenant / Client
The company that buys the platform. "Client" in prose, tenant_id on the wire. Every entity below is scoped to one tenant.
Program
The container for a client's entire referral operation. Exactly one per tenant, auto-provisioned at tenant creation; it is never a user-facing creation step. Holds program-wide defaults (attribution model, attribution window) and the Program Health Score.
Campaign
A discrete referral initiative with one Pulse, a schedule, a budget and one or more Variants. The unit the dashboard is organised around.
Variant
A configuration bundle inside a campaign: one Segment reference (who gets this deal) + reward configuration (what they get) + messaging (what they read) + priority (resolution order) + allocation weight (only between random segments). A Variant is not a Segment; it references one.
Default Variant
The Variant every campaign has. In a single-variant campaign it holds the whole configuration; in a multi-variant campaign it is the catch-all for participants who match no other variant's segment.
Segment
A reusable, tenant-scoped audience definition: a predicate over participant attributes ("plan in [enterprise]", "country = DE"), a materialised list, or a deterministic random split. Segments exist independently of campaigns and are shared across variants. The Segment is the filter; the Variant is the package delivered to whoever passes the filter.
Pulse
A workflow template bound to a campaign. It fixes the qualifying action the platform waits for (the Conversion), the tracked event that signals it, the entry condition, the reward-logic pattern, the timeout and the rollback behaviour, and which enrollment triggers are allowed. Nine Pulses exist (§6).
Playbook
A vertical preset curated by the platform team (not generated by AI): recommended Pulse, reward structure, enrollment trigger and segment strategy. The AI may recommend a Playbook; it never authors one.

People — The Two External Roles

A person outside the platform is in exactly one of two roles with respect to a given campaign, and may hold both roles with respect to different campaigns (or, after enrollment, with respect to the same campaign).

Participant
A person the client has enrolled in a campaign. Enrollment gives them a variant (frozen), a referral link, and the ability to refer and to earn. Participants are the client's own users; they are reached only through the in-app widget and emails. Replaces "referrer" everywhere except the wire path /v1/referrers.
Invitee
A person who arrived at the client's product through a participant's referral link. An invitee is anonymous at the first click, becomes identified when the client reports a signup (or an email-identified touch), and becomes converted when they complete the campaign's qualifying action. An invitee has no platform access and no credential. Replaces "referee" and "referred person".
R-CON-01An invitee becomes a participant only through an explicit client enrollment; nothing an invitee does on their own (clicking, signing up, paying) makes them a participant.

WhyEnrollment is a client decision (who may represent the brand, at which deal) and it is the moment the variant is resolved and frozen. If conversion or signup silently created participants, the platform would be resolving variants on people the client never chose and on attributes it does not have.

FitsKey constraint "selective, backend-owned enrollment"; R-PRT-01 (enrollment methods); R-SEG-07 (invitee-to-participant resolution); the enrollment trigger of each Pulse (§6) names the moment at which the client backend should call the enrollment API — it is never the platform acting.

ExampleAlice (participant) shares her link. Bob clicks it (invitee, anonymous), signs up (invitee, identified) and pays (invitee, converted). Bob is still not a participant. The client's backend, in its signup handler, calls POST /v1/referrers for Bob because the campaign's Pulse allows the signup enrollment trigger — that call makes Bob a participant of the campaign, with his own link.

R-CON-02A person is one platform record per tenant, whatever roles they hold. The invitee record Bob acquired when he clicked Alice's link and the participant record Bob acquires at enrollment are linked by identity stitching, never duplicated.

WhySelf-referral detection, dual-role bookkeeping (§7) and GDPR erasure all need "Bob" to be one subject. Two records for one person would let Bob refer his own open inbound referral and would split his data across two DSR responses.

FitsR-PRT-05 (dual-role ledgers), R-PRT-06 (find-or-revive on reactivation), six-key identity ladder (§10).

Tracked event
Any of the twelve external facts above, after it has passed ingestion (validation, dedup, trust enforcement, consent gating, server-side context). The superset.
Touch
A tracked event of the touch class: a pre-conversion interaction on the referral journey that carries attribution context (ref_code, click_id, session_id) and never carries revenue. Invitee-journey touches are what attribution models reduce over; participant-engagement touches (share, widget view) are never attribution inputs.
Invitation
A participant-initiated, platform-sent delivery of their referral link to a named invitee email (widget "invite by email"). Subject to the two-level invitation-channel setting (R-PRT-02).
Conversion
A tracked event of the conversion class (or a custom event the running Pulse recognises as its trigger) in which an invitee completes the qualifying action the campaign's Pulse is waiting for. The Conversion closes the Referral and triggers reward calculation. Which tracked event counts as "the" conversion depends on the Pulse (table below).
Domain event
A fact the platform produces about itself (referral.created, attribution.computed, reward.approved…). Never submitted from outside. Domain events are consequences of tracked events, not tracked events.
R-CON-03Touch is a strict subset of tracked event. A conversion is a tracked event but never a touch; a custom event is a tracked event but never a touch; a domain event is neither.

WhyAttribution answers "which participant's link brought this invitee here?" by reading the sequence of touches that preceded a conversion. If the conversion itself — or an arbitrary custom event — were a touch, three things break: (1) circularity: a last-touch model would credit the conversion to itself, and a late payment would silently steal credit from the click that caused it; (2) trust boundary: touches may come from the browser under a publishable key (low trust, no revenue), whereas conversions must come from the backend or a signed billing webhook (high trust, revenue) — one class cannot be allowed to impersonate the other; (3) retention and privacy: touches are high-volume, 7-day-ingest-bounded, pseudonymised journey data retained 24 months then aggregated, while conversions are financial records retained under legal basis. Keeping the classes separate lets Attribution scan a small, well-typed slice of the log and lets Compliance apply the right retention to each.

FitsEvent Model §3.1–§4 (three validated forms, ten types); §10 Touch Model; R-SDK-02 (publishable keys send touches only); §12 retention table.

ExampleDay 0 Alice shares her link on LinkedIn (touch, participant engagement — not an attribution input). Day 1 Bob clicks it (touch, invitee journey). Day 3 Bob opens Alice's invitation email (touch, invitee journey). Day 5 Bob signs up — the backend sends signup.completed (conversion class; under a Conversion Pulse it qualifies the referral, it does not convert it). Day 30 Bob pays — payment.completed (the conversion for a Conversion Pulse). Attribution reads the two invitee-journey touches; the first-touch model credits Alice. Day 40 the client sends custom.recorded{plan.upgraded} — a segmentation signal, stored, never part of the touch sequence.

The Relationship — Referral

Referral
The tracked relationship between one participant and one invitee within one campaign, opened by the invitee's first touch on the participant's link and run as one Temporal workflow instance. It carries the frozen attribution context (campaign, variant, participant, link) and advances through Pending → Qualified → Converted → Rewarded, or ends Expired (window elapsed) or Rejected (fraud / ineligible).
Referral link
The unique URL (and ref_code) issued to a participant at enrollment, bound to the resolved variant. Links can expire; expiry stops new referrals and leaves open ones untouched.
Attribution
The computation, at conversion time, of which referral (hence which participant) is credited, using the campaign's model (first-touch by default) over the invitee's touch sequence. Produces an immutable AttributionResult.
Reward · Payout
A Reward is the incentive instance earned on a converted referral (Pending → Held → Approved → Processing → Paid | Rejected | Reversed). A Payout is the batch that moves money for approved rewards whose hold has elapsed.
R-CON-04Referral states mean exactly this. Pending: opened by the invitee's landing click (referral.link.clicked), invitee anonymous. Qualified: the invitee is identified, the Pulse's entry condition is confirmed and the dedup passed — a real lead, waiting for the qualifying action. Converted: the qualifying action seen and validated. Rewarded: a reward instance approved. Completed: a recurring-structure referral finished normally (cycle cap or cancellation). Expired: window closed without conversion. Rejected: fraud or manual rejection. Every referral passes through Qualified; where identification is itself the qualifying action (Signup) both transitions happen in one workflow step and both events are emitted.

WhyQualified is the lead state: funnel analytics, lead webhooks and invitee dedup all need "attached to a known person, not yet converted". Emitting it even when it is immediate keeps "qualified" meaning the same thing in all nine Pulses. Completed exists because Expired and Rejected are both wrong for "finished normally" (R-RWD-03).

Fits§6 per-Pulse table (qualified when / converted when); R-CON-07 (only the landing click opens a referral); webhooks referral.qualified, referral.completed (§13); Event Model §5 referral family.

R-CON-07Only referral.link.clicked — the SDK observing the invitee land on the client's site — opens a referral. referral.page.viewed, referral.email.opened and referral.email.clicked append to an existing referral and never create one; an email click and the landing click it causes are one touch, merged by click_id.

WhyThe click is the first fact about a specific invitee: it starts the attribution window, creates the Temporal instance later signals are delivered to, runs the Referral checkpoint and fraud creation gate before any value is at stake, and gives analytics the top of the funnel. Opening at share is impossible (one share, many invitees); opening at signup loses the anonymous journey. The email-redirect click cannot open one because corporate mail scanners follow links and would create phantom referrals; only a real browser session on the client's site counts.

ExampleScenario: Bob invites Alice by email. The redirect records referral.email.clicked{click_id: c1} — no referral yet. Alice lands; the SDK fires referral.link.clicked{click_id: c1} → referral opened (Pending), the email click merged as touch 1's channel. A security scanner that followed the same link an hour earlier produced only an email.clicked that never became a referral.

Cross-Cutting Concepts

Eligibility
A decision function evaluated at one of five checkpoints (Entry, Referral, Conversion, Reward, Payout) returning allow / block + reason. It reads inputs (participant state, campaign state, caps, fraud score, trust) and never mutates them. Rules live in one Policy Decision Point (Segmentation & Eligibility); the checkpoints are enforced by the service that owns the moment.
Fraud signal · Fraud score
A signal is one observation (self-referral match, velocity spike, disposable email, device collision). The per-event fraud score (0–1) aggregates the signals present on that event plus the participant's history. Signals explain scores; scores drive checkpoint actions.
Trust score · Trust level
A per-participant, per-program score (0–100) summarising history, mapped to four levels (New, Established, Trusted, Advocate) that set payout limit, hold period, approval mode and fraud-check depth.
AI Recommendation
A proposed change or insight with rationale, data window and confidence. Never mutates state.
AI Decision
An AI output that does mutate state, allowed only inside pre-declared bounds (sub-threshold incentive tweak; fraud auto-block above the checkpoint threshold). Logged with its triggering Recommendation; reversible.
Human Override
An operator action that accepts, edits, rejects or reverts a Recommendation or Decision. Itself an audited domain event.
Program Health Score
A 0–100 composite per Program (funnel, revenue, fraud pressure, saturation), recomputed nightly; the signal the Insights Panel runs on (§11).

Event Ingestion vs Referral Workflow — Why Two Domains

R-DOM-01Event Ingestion is stateless and owns no business outcome; Referral Workflow is stateful and owns every referral outcome. A tracked event never produces a referral, reward or attribution decision inside Ingestion — it is classified, validated and forwarded, and the outcome is decided by the workflow that consumes it from the bus.

WhyThe two have opposite scaling and risk profiles. Ingestion is the most internet-exposed surface (browser traffic under publishable keys, retries, spikes, abuse) and must scale horizontally and fail without consequence. Referral Workflow runs long-lived Temporal workflows (a Renewal pulse lives for months) and must never be disturbed by an ingestion incident. Splitting them means a DDoS, a validation bug or a Redis outage at the edge never corrupts a running referral, and a slow attribution query never backs up the edge. It also keeps the trust boundary honest: Ingestion decides whether a fact is admissible (key type, schema, consent, dedup); Referral Workflow decides what the fact means.

FitsArchitecture §1.2 and §12.1–§12.2; R-SDK-02 (trust boundary at the edge); R-CON-03 (touch vs conversion classes are enforced at ingestion); Failure Model (ingestion degrades to "accept without dedup", workflows are durable).

ExampleBob clicks Alice's link. Ingestion checks the publishable key may send a touch, validates the schema, confirms the campaign is accepting events (availability cache), dedups on tenant_id + external_id, derives IP-hash/geo server-side and publishes touch.recorded. It does not know whether a referral exists. Referral Workflow consumes the event, finds no open referral for Bob on Alice's link, runs the Referral checkpoint and fraud creation gate, opens the Referral (Pending) and starts the Pulse workflow.

R-DOM-02Attribution is computed inside the Referral Workflow (between conversion validation and reward emission) and nowhere else. Analytics serves attribution results read-only; it never computes credit.

WhyAttribution sits on the money-critical path: the reward amount depends on it. Computing it in Analytics would put a payout decision behind an eventually-consistent, dashboard-loaded OLAP store (5–30 s lag), and would split "who is owed" between two services whose states could drift. Keeping it in the workflow makes it durable, ordered and auditable per referral.

Fits§10 (resolution ladder, models); Architecture §11 (attribution step inside the Referral Lifecycle workflow), DB Model referral_db.attribution_records replicated to ClickHouse.

Ownership Boundaries — Who Owns the Truth

R-DOM-03Each domain writes only its own database and exposes its truth through domain events and its public API. The open-referral lifecycle (Pending → Qualified → Converted) is owned by Referral Workflow; the moment a conversion is priced, Rewards & Payouts owns the monetary lifecycle (Pending → … → Paid). Analytics owns no lifecycle.

WhyThe classic referral-platform bug is reward state and referral state drifting apart in two services. One owner per lifecycle, with a single hand-over event (referral.converted), makes drift structurally impossible.

FitsArchitecture §2 "does not own" rows; DB Model (no cross-service foreign keys, ULIDs by value).

Cross-Domain Event Flow — Click to Payout

The canonical happy path for a Conversion Pulse, showing which domain acts at each step and the event that carries the baton. This is the backbone every Pulse workflow (§6) orchestrates.

R-DOM-05Event Ingestion has three ingress families, each with its own credential and none with another's: the public API (POST /v1/events, SDK endpoints — API keys), the provider receivers (/inbound/{provider}/{connection_id} — the provider's signature scheme, no API key) and the email-tracking endpoints (/t/o, /t/c — a token signed by Notifications, no API key). All three run the same admission pipeline; the receivers and tracking endpoints are deployed separately from the public API (ingestion-public, ingestion-inbound: one codebase, two deployments, separate ingress routes, rate limits and autoscaling). Receiver adapters translate protocol only — revenue normalisation, provider_customer_id, ref_code from metadata, raw discriminators such as billing_reason passed through — and never infer facts that need history (is_first_payment, is_reactivation are decided by the running Pulse workflow). No event and no call flows from Notifications to Event Ingestion.

WhyHosting the receivers in Notifications (whose job is the client's ecosystem, outbound) left provider conversions outside the admission pipeline — no Rules Guard, no 90-day dedup, no availability check — on the one event class that moves money. Hosting them in Ingestion closes that, but the public edge is also the DDoS-exposed surface: a spike on /v1/events must never delay a Stripe webhook that the provider will stop retrying after about three days, hence two deployments. Keeping adapters stateless keeps R-DOM-01 true: Ingestion decides admissibility, the workflow decides meaning.

FitsR-DOM-01, R-DOM-04; §9 ingress-credential table (R-SDK-07); R-SDK-04 (tracking endpoints); R-INT-02; Architecture §2.4/§2.8; API Contract §6.5.

R-DOM-04Every inbound tracked event carries a client-supplied idempotency key (external_id); every consumer is idempotent on its own composite key and tolerates at-least-once, out-of-order delivery. A late conversion.recorded arriving after campaign.paused is still processed.

WhyBrowsers retry, billing providers redeliver, SQS is at-least-once. Without end-to-end idempotency a single retried payment webhook would mint two rewards. Processing pipeline conversions after a pause honours the "existing pipeline still processed" budget rule (R-CMP-04).

FitsArchitecture §13 (dedup keys per service); Event Model §2 (external_id 90-day window); R-CMP-04.

Three families of actor touch the platform. Internal actors are the client's staff and the platform operator, authenticated through Ory and authorized through Keto. External actors are participants and invitees: they hold no credential and are never Keto subjects. System actors are machines — the client's backend, the SDK in a browser, billing-provider webhooks, integration service accounts, the AI agent and the platform's own workers.

R-SEG-01Segments are tenant-scoped and tenant-authored. Random Segments (Traffic Splitting)

R-SEG-02Traffic splitting between variants is done with random segments — a deterministic, sticky hash split (SHA-256(participant_id + seed) mod 100 < percentage) inside one mutual_exclusion_group. There is no separate experimentation framework or "test audience" entity.

WhyAn experiment is a comparison within a defined population; the population is a segment and the arms are random sub-segments of it. Implementing the split as a segment type reuses the resolution chain, the materialisation rules and the eligibility checkpoints instead of duplicating them.

Fits§6 A/B Testing (R-CMP-10); R-SEG-05 (allocation weight only between random segments); R-SEG-14 (segmentation precedes experimentation).

segment: {name: reward_test_control, type: random, assignment: {percentage: 50, seed: "camp_123_2026", sticky: true, mutual_exclusion_group: "reward_test"}} — properties: percentage (1–100), seed (reproducibility), sticky (stays in the same arm), mutual_exclusion_group (cannot be in two arms).

Variant Resolution Timing

R-SEG-03The variant is resolved once, at enrollment — the moment the client registers a person as a participant of a campaign (POST /v1/referrers, batch, CSV, CRM) — and frozen for that enrollment. It is never resolved at an invitee's click, and it never re-resolves silently afterwards.

WhyThe participant must know the deal before sharing. "Share and earn €50" is only honest if every qualified referral earns €50. Resolving at click would let two invitees of the same participant land on €50 and €15; resolving again later would change a reward the participant may already be advertising.

FitsKey constraint "enrollment-time variant resolution"; R-SEG-10 (enrichment never re-resolves); R-CON-05 (clock 1); Architecture §2.2.

Variant Allocation Fallback Chain

R-SEG-04At enrollment the variants are evaluated in descending priority; the first variant whose segment matches is assigned. If none matches, the Default Variant is assigned. If the campaign has no Default Variant (impossible — see R-CMP-02), the person is ineligible.

WhyA deterministic, ordered chain is explainable to the client ("why did Alice get Variant B?") and reproducible for audit; a weighted draw between overlapping segments is neither.

ExampleCampaign variants: Enterprise (P1, €100), SMB (P2, €25), Default (P99, €15). Alice enrolls with plan = enterprise → Enterprise. Carol enrolls with plan = starter → matches neither rule → Default €15. Single-variant campaigns skip evaluation entirely.

R-SEG-05Two non-random segments that both match a participant are an authoring overlap, resolved by priority. allocation_weight is not a tie-breaker: it is meaningful only between variants bound to random segments of one mutual_exclusion_group, where the hash bucket — not priority — decides.

WhyMixing "priority wins" and "weight draws" in one chain made two documents disagree; one rule for rules, one rule for random.

FitsAPI Contract §3.3 (allocation_weight scoped to random segments).

What Data Drives Resolution

R-SEG-06Variant resolution reads only the participant attributes the client supplies at enrollment (plan, mrr, country, account_age, tags) and the memberships materialised from them. The passive data the platform holds about an invitee's journey — email, IP-derived geo, device fingerprint — is captured for attribution and fraud and is never used to resolve a variant.

WhyEmail identifies a person, not a business tier. IP geo is coarse and spoofable; a country rule needs the asserted billing country. A device fingerprint is a fraud signal; using it to set a reward would be unreliable and a privacy problem. Only the client's own system knows the facts a deal should depend on.

FitsR-SEG-08 (thin attributes → Default Variant); §12 purpose limitation (fraud signals are collected for fraud only).

Invitee Becomes Participant — Fresh Resolution, No Inheritance

R-SEG-07An invitee who is later enrolled (R-CON-01) is resolved at that enrollment, on their own attributes, exactly like any other person — never before, and never from the variant of the participant who referred them.

WhyBefore enrollment an invitee has no variant at all: variant resolution is an enrollment-time computation and the invitee has not been enrolled. At enrollment there is nothing to inherit either — a participant's variant encodes the deal that participant was offered, sized to their segment. Passing it down would let a low-value participant mint high-value deals for everyone they refer, break per-segment budgeting, and make a person's reward depend on who referred them rather than who they are — the opposite of the attribute-based, stable-€50 invariant.

FitsR-SEG-03, R-SEG-08; §6 enrollment triggers (which name the moment the client should enroll an invitee).

ExampleAlice (Variant A, €50) refers Bob. Bob converts. The client enrolls Bob. Bob is evaluated against the variants' segments with plan = starter → Variant B, €25. Bob's deal is €25 because of Bob's attributes; Alice's €50 is untouched.

Missing Attributes Are the Normal Case

R-SEG-08A missing attribute evaluates as "does not match" — never as a match — and the participant follows the fallback chain to the Default Variant. The platform enrolls with whatever attributes exist (possibly none) and never blocks enrollment to wait for an attribute.

WhyIn the main acquisition flows the attribute is simply not known at enrollment: a participant sharing on social media cannot supply their friend's age; a signup captures email and maybe a name. Blocking those enrollments loses the client brand-new users, the opposite of the product's job; guessing silently would set a reward on fiction.

ExampleCampaign targets age < 25 → Variant A (€30) and age ≥ 25 → Variant B (€20). Dana signs up through a social link; the client knows no age and enrolls her anyway. Neither rule matches → Default Variant, Dana starts referring immediately. Nothing was guessed, nobody was blocked.

R-SEG-09Each segment carries on_missing_attribute: default | block, default default. block is reserved for compliance gates (legal eligibility) and can only be set with segments:write; it is never the silent default.

WhyWhether "unknown" should fall through or block depends on what the attribute is for. A targeting attribute (which deal) can fall through harmlessly; a legal gate (age-restricted product — alcohol, gambling) cannot: enrolling an unknown-age person may be unlawful, and the client must choose that trade of conversion for safety explicitly.

FitsAPI Contract §3.4; DB Model segments.on_missing_attribute; Entry checkpoint below.

Progressive Attribute Enrichment

R-SEG-10Participant attributes are enrichable over the participant's lifetime (participant.attributes_updated, an audited event). Enrichment affects future enrollments; it never re-resolves an existing enrollment. Moving an enrolled participant to a newly matched variant is an explicit operator- or rule-approved action ("promote at next campaign cycle"), never automatic.

WhySilent re-resolution would change a reward the participant may already be advertising, breaking R-SEG-03. Late data is welcome; it just applies going forward.

R-SEG-11In acquisition-heavy flows (social shares, invitee-to-participant enrollments, any signup-time enrollment with thin data) the Default Variant is the most-used variant, not a rare catch-all. Clients size its reward and budget as a primary lever; the Creation Assistant surfaces its economics first.

WhyA direct consequence of R-SEG-08: thin attributes are common, so the Default Variant's economics can dominate the program.

Real-Time vs Batch Evaluation

R-SEG-13Anything evaluated at the enrollment decision must be answerable in real time: static and rule-based segments are evaluated live; AI-generated and predictive segments are materialised into membership tables by a nightly batch and read in real time at enrollment.

WhyThe read must always be fast even when the compute is heavy, and the segment a participant matched at enrollment must not shift mid-flight — the batch materialisation is what makes the "stable €50" invariant hold for AI-driven segments too.

Why Segmentation Precedes Experimentation

R-SEG-14The experiment framework (§6) is built on segments, never beside them: an experiment is a mutually exclusive random partition of an already-segmented population.

Why(1) A 50/50 reward test only means something if both arms draw from the same audience — otherwise it compares enterprise to SMB and the result is noise. (2) Variant resolution happens at enrollment, so the arm must be assignable at enrollment, which requires the participant to be classified first — you cannot split a set you have not defined. (3) Revenue-first analysis needs a stable denominator: "Variant B lifted revenue per participant 12%" requires "participant" to mean the same cohort across arms.

A campaign is the unit a Campaign Manager runs: one Pulse (what the invitee must do), a schedule, a budget, and one or more variants (who gets which deal). A Pulse is the workflow template that every referral of the campaign executes; nine exist and they share one skeleton.

Enrollment Trigger (Per-Pulse)

When should a new person become a participant of this campaign — at their signup, at their own conversion, or only by hand? This is the enrollment trigger. It names a moment for the client's integration; it never makes the platform enroll anyone (R-CON-01).

R-CMP-10The enrollment trigger is a property of the Pulse: the Pulse declares the allowed values (signup, on_self_conversion, manual) and a default; a Playbook may preset one of the allowed values; a campaign may override within the allowed set. WhyWhether widget-at-signup is safe depends on the Pulse's value and fraud profile, which is intrinsic to the mechanic regardless of client or vertical. Invitation Channel — Two Levels

R-PRT-02"Invite by email" (platform-sent invitations, R-CON definition) is governed at two levels. R-PRT-03Enrollment time governs "can invite"; the participant's invitees' conversions govern "can be paid"; the participant's own conversion governs nothing by default. The participant record stores both enrolled_at and self_converted_at (nullable).

WhyA person enrolled at signup can hold a live link and invite immediately; their earnings read €0 because nobody they invited has converted yet, not because they have not paid. Storing both timestamps makes the enrolled-but-never-self-converted cohort measurable — the cohort the signup trigger deliberately creates.

FitsR-CON-05; R-CMP-10 (triggers); R-SEG-03 (resolution stays pinned to clock 1 and frozen).

R-PRT-04Optional lever, off by default: a campaign may hold a participant's earned rewards (hold_reason = self_conversion_gate) until the participant's own conversion clears; participant.self_converted releases the hold.

WhyBoth a conversion nudge ("€40 waiting — subscribe to unlock") and a fraud dampener for high-value, low-verification Pulses (Switch-Up). Not the default because it feels unfair to a genuine advocate who brought paying customers while on a free plan and it suppresses sharing; prefer trust limits and ordinary holds for routine risk.

R-PRT-05Dual-role bookkeeping: a person who is both an open invitee (their inbound referral maturing toward another participant's reward) and a participant (their outbound link live) has two ledgers that are never netted, and the self-referral guard covers the loop — they cannot route their own outbound referral into their own open inbound one.

WhyNetting would hide fraud and mis-state both participants' earnings; the loop is the simplest self-referral there is.

FitsR-CON-02 (one record per person); Referral checkpoint self-referral rule (§5); R-PRT-10 fraud signals.

R-PRT-06Reactivation enrollment finds or revives the existing participant record — rehydrating prior trust level, limits and holds — and never creates a second record for the same person. Only genuinely new people create a participant.

WhyA churned-and-returned user may carry prior referrals, holds or a fraud history; a fresh record would launder it.

Trust Model

The trust model is a single per-participant score (0–100) that decides how much money the platform is willing to risk on that participant right now, by setting four controls: monthly payout limit, hold period, reward-approval mode and fraud-check depth. It starts tight and loosens as clean behaviour accumulates.

R-PRT-08Trust is computed by one owner — Referral Workflow, which holds the Participant aggregate — and scoped to (participant × program): tenant-isolated, never carried from one client to another. All campaigns of a program feed one score. Recomputed on every relevant event (reward approved, fraud flag, verification, refund); every level change emits participant.trust_changed. MVP bypasses the score with fixed rules for everyone: manual review, 7-day hold, €1,000/month. Lot 1 activates the levels.

WhyOne scoring logic keeps risk rules consistent, versioned and auditable; per-program scope keeps one client's fraud history out of another's data (GDPR separation). The accepted cost: a trustworthy person starts cold at every new client.

FitsArchitecture §2.5; DB participants.trust_score / trust_tier; R-RWD-05 (approval function reads the level); R-PRT-10 (fraud outcomes are inputs).

Fraud Detection

R-PRT-10Fraud detection is per event: signals present on the event plus the participant's history are weighted into a score 0–1, and the score drives a checkpoint-specific action — below 0.3 proceed, 0.3–0.7 manual review (reward Held, participant Flagged), above the checkpoint threshold auto-block + alert. Thresholds tighten as money approaches: creation > 0.8, conversion and reward > 0.7, payout > 0.6. The AI service scores; the owning service enforces (Referral at creation/conversion, Rewards at reward/payout). Every outcome is recorded as an event and feeds the trust score.

WhyFraud asks "is this event suspicious?"; trust asks "how much do we trust this person overall?". Keeping them distinct lets a trusted participant still have one bad event blocked, and lets a new participant build trust from clean events. Stricter thresholds closer to payout minimise exposure where reversal is hardest.

FitsArchitecture §4 (AI tiers — rules in real time, no LLM); R-SEG-12 checkpoints; R-RWD-05; R-PRT-07 state transitions; audit (§12).

8. Rewards

A reward is the incentive instance a participant (and, in two-sided variants, the invitee) earns on a converted referral. Its configuration lives on the variant; its lifecycle is owned by Rewards & Payouts; its approval is a deterministic function of fraud score, trust level, amount and budget.

WhyType and structure are independent: a cash reward can be fixed or recurring; a feature unlock can be milestone-based. Modelling them as one enum produced an unmaintainable matrix.

Recurring Earnings and the Completed State

R-RWD-03A referral converts exactly once. If the variant's reward structure is recurring, the referral stays Rewarded with its workflow open: each subsequent subscription.renewed for the same invitee creates one more reward instance (reward.earned{cycle_number: n}) at the current cycle value, until cycles_cap is reached or a subscription.cancelled arrives — then the referral enters Completed (completion_reason ∈ cap_reached | cancelled). Paid cycles are never clawed back on cancellation; a refund of one cycle reverses that cycle's reward only; renewals received after Completed are logged and ignored. For non-recurring structures Rewarded is terminal and Completed is never entered.

Why"Earn many times" is a property of the reward structure, not of a Pulse, and must work on every Pulse that allows recurring. One referral per cycle would break the durable revenue key (§10); a loop back to Qualified would send a converted referral into a lead state. A reactivated subscription after Completed must not silently resume paying — that is what the Reactivation Pulse is for.

FitsR-CON-04 (Completed); R-CMP-08 saga; R-CMP-09 (subscription.cancelled is a tracked event); §10 revenue-first attribution; DB referrals.status += completed, rewards.cycle_number; Event Model referral.completed.

ExampleAlice (Agencies variant, 10% recurring, 12 cycles) refers Bob at €100/month. Day 20 first payment → Converted → Rewarded, cycle 1 €10. Day 50 renewal → cycle 2 €10. Carol (Default, fixed €50) refers Dave: one reward at Dave's first payment, referral terminal at Rewarded; Dave's later renewals still count toward Carol's attributed MRR in analytics but create no reward.

Two-Sided Rewards

R-RWD-02A variant may define an invitee-side reward in addition to the participant's. The invitee reward is created on the same conversion, follows the same approval gates, and is fulfilled through the client's system (account credit, discount, trial) via the reward.approved webhook — the platform never pays an invitee cash.

WhyThe invitee has no payout method and no relationship with the platform; non-monetary fulfilment inside the client's product is the only surface they have.

Reward Lifecycle

R-RWD-04Reward states are Pending → Held → Approved → Processing → Paid | Rejected | Reversed. The creation event on the bus and the outbound webhook share one name, reward.earned. Held is a first-class state entered for a named reason (fraud_review_band, over_trust_ceiling, participant_flagged, manual_mode, ai_hold, self_conversion_gate). Approved rewards can be Reversed on refund, chargeback or confirmed fraud, including after Paid (clawback = negative ledger entry).

WhyA reward that was "calculated" and one that was "earned" are the same fact under two names; one name end to end removes a translation table. Naming the hold reason makes the review queue explainable and lets the self-conversion gate release the right holds.

FitsDB rewards.status, hold_reason; Event Model §5.2; API §6.1.

Reward Approval Decision Function

R-RWD-05Approval is a deterministic, audited function of four inputs: fraud score, trust level, amount and remaining budget. In order: fraud score above the checkpoint threshold → Blocked + alert; fraud score 0.3–0.7 → Held (review); amount above the trust level's auto-approve ceiling → Held (human approval); budget remaining below amount → Blocked ("budget depleted"); otherwise Approved — instantly at Advocate, after the hold period for every other level. MVP overrides everything with Held + manual review + 7-day hold + €1,000/month.

WhyApproval is the first irreversible financial commitment; a fixed evaluation order makes every decision reproducible for audit and for the review queue's explanation.

9. Tracking & SDK

Two integration surfaces feed the platform: the JavaScript SDK in the client's product (publishable key, low trust, touches only) and server-side sources (the client's backend with a secret key, or the billing provider's webhooks through the platform's receivers — high trust, conversions). The server side is the attribution backbone; the SDK is an enhancement layer.

SDK Architecture

R-SDK-02Trust boundary at the edge: a publishable key may submit touch events only; conversion and custom events require the secret key (events from backend) or arrive through the billing-provider receiver (events from billing provider). IP, user agent and geo on any SDK event are derived server-side; client-claimed values are ignored. Any API key reaching a CRUD, reward, payout or analytics endpoint is refused (403).

WhyThe browser is untrusted: anything it sends can be fabricated. Touches are cheap to fake and worthless without a conversion; conversions move money and must come from a source the client controls server-side. R-CON-03 depends on this boundary.

FitsEvent Model §3.2; Architecture §12.1 (Business Rules Guard); R-DOM-01, R-DOM-05.

Identity Ladder — Resolution Priority

R-ATT-03Every conversion is linked to an open referral by one deterministic six-key ladder, tried in order, stopping at the first hit: (1) explicit ref_code in the payload — URL, cookie via RefRev.getAttribution(), or billing-provider metadata; (2) provider_customer_id already linked to an invitee holding an open referral (events from billing provider); (3) invitee_external_id already linked (events from backend); (4) click_id correlated to the originating landing click; (5) session_id — same-session touches carried the code; (6) invitee_email_hash matched to an open invitation or an email-identified touch; none → organic. Design Principles

R-AI-01Five principles bind every AI feature: embedded (in the flow, not a separate chat), human-in-the-loop (risky or irreversible actions require approval), explainable (every output carries its reasoning, data window and confidence), overridable (a human can always reverse), privacy-safe (pseudonymised features; no PII needed for most features; no training on tenant data — inference only under provider DPAs).

WhyClients delegate money and audience decisions to the platform; explainability and reversibility are what make that delegation acceptable to a Campaign Manager and defensible to a regulator (§12, automated-decision audit).

R-AI-02The AI service never writes operational records. A Recommendation becomes real only when an operator accepts it (POST /v1/recommendations/{id}/accept), which replays the underlying action through the owning service's public API under the operator's permissions. The two autonomous Decision paths execute through a dedicated service subject, service:ai-optimizer, holding only variants:write bounded by the campaign's optimisation bounds, and the fraud path, which emits fraud.flagged for the owning service to enforce; both are logged with their triggering Recommendation and the bounds satisfied.

WhyOne write path per record keeps ownership (R-DOM-03) intact and makes "who changed this reward and why" answerable from the audit log alone.

FitsAPI §3.12/§3.14; §4 permission table; R-RWD-05; §12 decision audit.

R-AI-03AI needs no login to client systems. Every feature runs on public website data, brand inputs the client provides, or the platform's own event data.

Campaign Creation Assistant

Analysed, public data only: pricing page → reward range; features → value propositions for messaging; industry signals → Playbook; testimonials → social proof; company-size signals → segment strategy. The assistant reads the tenant's attribute vocabulary and propensity data to derive the CAC ceiling, saturation pacing, targeting and hold defaults, and proposes three configurations; accepting one materialises a draft campaign, its variants and segments as the operator (R-SEG-01). It never activates or budgets a campaign. Behaviour, prompts and derivations are in the Placement and Interview Script documents.

Output: three variants — HTML/CSS, React, Vue, Web Component, responsive — with copy and previews. Human selection and approval are required; the AI never deploys a widget.

Incentive Optimisation
